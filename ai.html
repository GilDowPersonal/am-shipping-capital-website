Eight applications / One connected journey

From possibility
to a practical workflow.

A proposed AI direction for Am Shipping Capital, from the first inquiry to the final invoice.

These are opportunities to evaluate, not installed systems or verified company capabilities. The company's actual processes, software, and data access would need to be confirmed first.

01 / Inquiries & documents ↘ 02 / Shipment operations ↘ 03 / Customers & knowledge ↘ 04 / Finance & decisions ↘ 05 / Integration & rollout ↘

Automation follows defined rules. AI interprets unstructured information. An agent coordinates a bounded sequence of tool-assisted tasks. None replaces accountable human judgment.

01 / Before the shipment

A clearer start.

Application 01 / Document intelligence

Turn paperwork into usable information.

Extract shipment references, cargo details, weights, and dates from booking requests, commercial invoices, packing lists, and bills of lading. Compare fields, flag missing information, and prepare a structured record with a link back to each source.

Integration & human review

Connect: an approved mailbox or document store to OCR, field validation, and a draft record in the transport management system (TMS). Treat document text as data, never as instructions to the agent. An operator checks uncertain extractions and discrepancies before any operational record is committed.

Application 02 / Inquiry & quotation assistant

Build the brief before the quote.

Read an incoming inquiry, identify the route and cargo requirements, and draft the questions needed to complete it. Retrieve approved, current rate data to assemble a quote worksheet with its assumptions, validity dates, and exclusions.

Integration & human review

Connect: email, the customer relationship management system (CRM), and an approved rate source. Missing or expired prices go to the commercial team, never to a model-generated estimate. A person approves pricing, terms, and every outbound quotation; the agent cannot book capacity or commit the company.

02 / During the journey

See the change.
Support the response.

Application 03 / Shipment visibility agent

Bring milestones into one view.

Collect available carrier and terminal events, match them to shipment references, and build a timestamped timeline. Highlight stale information, missed milestones, and changes to a source-provided estimated arrival.

Integration & human review

Connect: licensed carrier or terminal APIs, EDI messages, and the TMS, where available. Preserve source timestamps, deduplicate repeated events, and label estimates as estimates. Unavailable feeds remain visibly unavailable; an agent must not invent a location or promise an arrival time.

Application 04 / Exception coordination agent

Give exceptions an owner.

When a milestone is missed or a document is missing, assemble the relevant facts, open a case, and route it to the right team. Prepare a next-step checklist and draft a customer update, with follow-up reminders tied to the case.

Integration & human review

Connect: shipment events and approved rules to a case-management tool and internal task queue. An operations lead approves rerouting, bookings, extra charges, and external messages. If a tool fails or sources conflict, stop the action and escalate with the evidence rather than silently continue.

03 / People & information

An informed team.
A clearer conversation.

Application 05 / Customer communication assistant

Make each update easier to prepare.

Summarize a customer's shipment status, draft a plain-language response, and translate it when needed. Draw only on records the user is authorized to access, showing the source and freshness of every operational statement.

Integration & human review

Connect: a permission-filtered shipment lookup, CRM, and helpdesk. Verify the customer's identity and shipment access before retrieving information. Customer-service staff review and send drafts; complaints, financial commitments, and unclear answers move to a person.

Application 06 / Internal knowledge assistant

Find the process, not another inbox thread.

Help staff find approved procedures, document requirements, and internal handover instructions. Answer with linked passages from current material, and make it clear when the answer is not covered or needs a specialist.

Integration & human review

Connect: a versioned, access-controlled knowledge library to source-grounded retrieval. Keep customer-specific records separate from general procedures. Compliance specialists own regulatory interpretation, dangerous-goods decisions, and customs submissions; a generated answer is not clearance or legal advice.

04 / The commercial picture

Know what needs
a closer look.

Use AI to surface discrepancies and explain the record. Keep financial authority and operational decisions with the people responsible.

Application 07 / Invoice reconciliation

Check the charge against the agreement.

Compare supplier invoices with approved quotes, booking records, and available shipment events. Flag duplicate invoices, unexpected surcharges, currency differences, or disputed storage and detention charges for finance review.

Integration & human review

Connect: invoice intake, approved contracts, TMS records, and the accounting system in read-only or draft mode. Preserve agreed calculation rules and exchange-rate dates. Finance staff resolve discrepancies and approve posting or payment; the agent cannot change bank details or release funds.

Application 08 / Operations intelligence

Turn the weekly report into a useful brief.

Summarize open exceptions, response backlogs, recurring document issues, and costs from approved records. Let managers drill from each observation to its underlying data, rather than rely on an unsupported narrative.

Integration & human review

Connect: approved TMS, CRM, and finance reporting views. Calculate metrics in defined queries, then use AI to explain the results. Show coverage, missing values, and the reporting period. Management validates the interpretation before changing routes, staffing, or commercial policy.

05 / Integration, not another island

Connect to the workflow.
Keep control.

Start with the tools people already use. Confirm the real system landscape and access rights before choosing a model or agent framework.

One bounded workflow, end to end.

  1. Receive a real eventAn approved mailbox receives a shipment inquiry. Store its source and attachment references, and match it to an authorized customer record.
  2. Interpret, then validateAI extracts the brief; deterministic rules check required fields and units. Low-confidence fields and missing details become review tasks.
  3. Use narrowly scoped toolsA bounded agent retrieves permitted records and prepares a draft. Use an API, webhook, EDI feed, or controlled import that the actual system supports, with duplicate protection and an audit trail.
  4. Approve before committingAn accountable person checks the evidence and approves the specific write or message. Log the decision and result; failed actions enter a visible review queue.

Controls belong in the design.

Limit each agent to the minimum records and tools it needs. Keep credentials out of prompts, encrypt sensitive information, agree retention rules, and assess provider data use before sharing customer or cargo data.

Treat emails, documents, and external feeds as untrusted input. They cannot grant permissions, change an agent's instructions, or authorize a booking, payment, or disclosure. Enforce approval gates in the application, not just in a prompt.

Keep source links, tool calls, reviewer actions, and outcomes traceable. When data is missing, an answer is uncertain, or a connection fails, show that state and hand over to a person.

This website explains proposed workflows. It does not access company systems, process real shipment data, or run autonomous agents.

A sensible starting point

Prove one useful
workflow first.

Begin with inquiry and document intake in draft-only mode, using approved, representative records and an operator review queue.

This is a proposed pilot, not a deployment commitment. Expansion depends on the evidence and the team's approval.

  1. Map & baselineAgree the process owner, access scope, representative cases, current handling time, and error definitions.
  2. Run alongside the teamKeep outputs separate from production decisions. Measure extraction accuracy, missed issues, review effort, and handling time against the same baseline.
  3. Review before expandingSet acceptance criteria with the team, investigate failures, and rehearse disabling the integration. Only add narrowly permitted actions after sign-off; do not promise savings before measuring them.
